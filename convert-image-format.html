Convert Image Format changes an image from one file type to another — JPG, PNG, or WebP — without needing a design app. It's for the times a specific format is required: a platform that only accepts JPG, a design tool that needs PNG transparency, or simply wanting the smaller file sizes WebP tends to offer.

How this tool works

You pick a target format from a dropdown — JPG, PNG, or WebP — and, for JPG or WebP, a quality level. The tool draws your image onto an in-memory canvas at its original dimensions, then re-encodes it using the browser's own Canvas API into the format you chose. PNG is a lossless format, so the quality setting only has an effect when your target is JPG or WebP, both of which support adjustable compression; converting to PNG simply produces the most faithful possible copy in that format. The result downloads with a file extension matching your chosen format.

How to convert an image's format

  1. Drop your image on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Choose your target format — JPG, PNG, or WebP.
  3. If converting to JPG or WebP, choose a quality level.
  4. Click Convert and download the result, re-encoded locally in your browser.

Privacy and local processing

Your image stays on your device throughout. Redrawing it onto a canvas and re-encoding it into the new format both happen inside your browser — there's no upload step, so OnlineToolsWeb never sees the image. Once the page has loaded, conversion keeps working the same way offline.

Practical limitations

Converting to JPG discards transparency, since JPG has no alpha channel — any transparent areas in a source PNG or WebP will be filled in. Converting to a lossy format (JPG or WebP) at a low quality setting can introduce visible compression artifacts, particularly on images with sharp edges or fine detail. This tool doesn't handle HEIC source files — for those, use the dedicated HEIC to JPG tool. Very large or high-resolution source images take a little longer to redraw and re-encode, especially on phones or older devices with less processing power.

Common ways people use this tool

Typical uses include converting a PNG screenshot to JPG to shrink it before emailing, converting product photos to WebP to speed up a website, turning a JPG into a PNG when a design tool needs transparency support, and standardizing a mix of image formats into one consistent format before uploading a batch somewhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which formats can I convert between?

You can convert to JPG, PNG, or WebP, choosing your target format from a dropdown. The source image can be any common browser-supported image format — it doesn't need to already be one of those three.

Does the quality setting affect PNG output?

No. PNG is a lossless format, so the quality dropdown only makes a difference when converting to JPG or WebP, both of which support adjustable lossy compression. Converting to PNG ignores the quality setting since PNG doesn't have an equivalent control.

What happens to transparency when converting PNG to JPG?

JPG doesn't support transparency, so any transparent areas in a source PNG will be filled in when you convert to JPG. If you need to keep transparency, convert to WebP or PNG instead, both of which support it.

Does converting to WebP actually reduce file size?

Often, yes — WebP generally achieves smaller file sizes than JPG or PNG at comparable visual quality, which is part of why it's become common for websites. The exact savings depend on the specific image, so it's worth comparing the result against your original.

Can I convert HEIC images here?

This tool handles common browser-supported formats like JPG, PNG, and WebP as sources. For HEIC files specifically (the format many iPhones save photos in), use the dedicated HEIC to JPG tool instead.

Can I convert image formats on my phone?

Yes, the tool works the same way in a mobile browser, including the quality control for JPG and WebP output.
