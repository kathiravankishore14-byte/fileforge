PPT to Text converter — pull the words out of any slide deck

A slide deck is a terrible place to keep text you actually need to use. You can't search it properly, you can't paste it into a document without dragging in fonts and boxes, and reading fifty slides just to find one sentence is a waste of an afternoon. A PPT to Text converter solves that in one step: hand it a .pptx, get back a clean .txt file with every word, in slide order.

How to convert PPT to text online free

  1. Add your PowerPoint file. Drag your .pptx onto the box above, or tap Browse files to choose it. The file is opened in your browser — it is never uploaded.
  2. The converter reads each slide in order. It walks through slide 1, 2, 3 and so on, collecting the text from every title, bullet, text box and table it finds.
  3. Download your .txt file. You get a plain text file you can open in Notepad, TextEdit, Word, Google Docs, or paste anywhere you like.

What the text file actually looks like

The output is deliberately simple and readable. Each slide's text is written out under its own header, so you always know where a line came from:

  • Slide markers. Every slide starts with a --- Slide 1 ---, --- Slide 2 --- line, so a 60-slide deck stays navigable.
  • Original slide order. Slides are processed in their real deck order, not the order shapes happen to be stored in the file.
  • All on-slide text. Titles, bullet points, text boxes and table cells are all collected.
  • Plain .txt, nothing proprietary. No fonts, no layout, no formatting to strip out later — just the words.

What people use a PPT to Text converter for

  • Making notes from lecture slides. Students turn a term's worth of decks into searchable text they can revise from, summarise or print.
  • Feeding a deck into an AI tool. ChatGPT, Claude and similar tools work far better with clean text than with a slide file — this is the fastest way to get there.
  • Translation. Extract the text, translate it, then rebuild the deck — much quicker than editing inside PowerPoint box by box.
  • Word counts and proofreading. Copy-editors and reviewers want the words in one place, not spread across slides.
  • Repurposing a deck. Turning a presentation into a blog post, report, script or handout starts with getting the text out.
  • Searching and archiving. A .txt file is indexable and searchable by your operating system in a way a .pptx really isn't.

What it does not do

Being straight about the limits saves you a wasted upload:

  • Speaker notes are not included. PowerPoint stores notes separately from the slides, and this tool reads the slides.
  • Text inside images isn't read. If a slide has a screenshot with words in it, those words are pixels, not text. That needs OCR, not extraction.
  • Formatting is not preserved. That's the point — you're asking for plain text. Bold, colours, fonts and positions are all dropped.
  • .ppt (the old format) isn't accepted. Save it as .pptx first in PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice Impress.

Why use this converter instead of copying slides by hand

Selecting every text box across forty slides and pasting them one at a time is the kind of task that takes twenty minutes and produces mistakes — skipped boxes, lost ordering, stray formatting pasted along with the words. A converter does the full deck in one pass and keeps the slide order intact. It is also the only sane option when you have several decks to get through.

Free, private and browser-based

  • No signup or email. Most PPT to Text converters want an address before they hand over your file. This one doesn't.
  • Nothing is uploaded. The conversion runs locally in your browser, which matters when the deck is an unreleased pitch, a client report or unpublished research.
  • No file limits or watermarks. Convert as many decks as you need.
  • Works anywhere. Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iPhone — any modern browser, nothing to install.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PPT to Text converter free to use?

Yes. Extracting text from a PowerPoint on OnlineToolsWeb is completely free, with no trial, no credit card and no signup. Optional premium features may be added later for advanced workflows like batch processing, but this tool's core functionality stays free.

How do I extract text from a PowerPoint online?

Drop your .pptx file into the box on this page. The converter reads each slide in order, pulls out every piece of text on it, and hands you a .txt file to download. The whole thing happens in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

What does the downloaded text file look like?

Plain text, in slide order, with a clear "--- Slide 1 ---", "--- Slide 2 ---" header before each slide's content. That makes it easy to see which words came from which slide, and easy to search, paste or edit afterwards.

Does it extract speaker notes as well?

No. This tool reads the text that appears on the slides themselves — titles, bullet points, text boxes and tables. Speaker notes are stored separately inside a PowerPoint file and are not included in the output.

Can I convert an old .ppt file to text?

This converter accepts the modern .pptx format. If you have an older .ppt file, open it in PowerPoint, Google Slides or LibreOffice Impress and save it as .pptx first, then bring it back here.

Will it read text that is inside an image on a slide?

No. Text baked into a picture or screenshot is part of the image, not the slide's text, so it can't be extracted this way. Only real, editable text on the slide is picked up.

Is my presentation safe when I convert PPT to text?

Yes. Your deck is opened and read entirely on your own device and is never uploaded to a server, so confidential pitch decks, internal reports and unpublished research stay private.

Does the PPT to Text converter work on mobile?

Yes. It runs in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge on phones and tablets exactly as it does on a laptop, with nothing to install.
