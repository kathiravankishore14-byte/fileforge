Accepts: PDF files

Split PDF breaks one PDF file into several smaller ones, based on page ranges you type in yourself. It's built for pulling a specific chapter out of a report, separating a scanned batch into individual documents, or dividing a long file into pieces small enough to send or file separately.

How this tool works

You describe how to split the file using a simple comma-separated range syntax — for example 1-3,4-6,7. Each group separated by a comma becomes one output file: the tool reads your source PDF's pages in your browser, copies the pages named in each group into a brand-new PDF, and repeats that for every group you listed. Pages within a group are always assembled in ascending order, duplicate page numbers are collapsed automatically, and any page number that falls outside your document's actual range is simply skipped rather than causing an error. Once every group has been built into its own file, if there's more than one, they're packaged together into a single ZIP so you can download everything at once.

How to split a PDF file

  1. Drop your PDF on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Type your page ranges as comma-separated groups, such as 1-3,4-6,7.
  3. Click Split. Each group is copied into its own PDF, entirely in your browser.
  4. Download the result — a ZIP of your split files, or a single PDF if you only entered one group.

Privacy and local processing

Your PDF stays on your device throughout. Reading the source file, copying pages, and building each new PDF all happen in your browser's own JavaScript engine — nothing is uploaded, and OnlineToolsWeb never sees or stores the file's contents. Once the page has loaded, splitting keeps working the same way offline too.

Practical limitations

Encrypted or password-protected PDFs can't be opened without their password, so they need to go through Unlock PDF before they can be split here. Because your whole document and every output file are held in your browser's memory while it works, splitting a very large PDF — hundreds of pages, or pages made of high-resolution scanned images — into many small groups can use a noticeable amount of memory and run more slowly, especially on phones or older devices with less RAM. Page ranges outside your document's actual page count are silently ignored rather than flagged, so it's worth checking the page count shown for your file before entering ranges.

Common ways people use this tool

Typical uses include pulling a single chapter or section out of a long report, separating a multi-document scan (like several forms scanned in one pass) back into individual files, breaking a large PDF into smaller pieces to fit an email or upload size limit, and extracting just the pages someone actually needs to review out of a much longer document.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I specify which pages go in each split file?

Type a comma-separated list of page groups, where each group can be a single page or a range: for example 1-3,4-6,7 produces three files — pages 1 through 3, pages 4 through 6, and page 7 on its own. Each comma-separated group becomes one output PDF.

What happens if I enter a page number that doesn't exist in the file?

Any page number outside your PDF's actual page count is silently ignored rather than causing an error. If a group ends up with no valid pages at all, it's simply skipped, so it's worth double-checking your ranges against the page count shown for your file.

Can I split a PDF into more than two files?

Yes — there's no limit on how many groups you enter. Each comma-separated group in your page range becomes its own file, so a 20-page document could be split into 20 single-page files, a handful of chapters, or any mix in between.

Do I download one file or several?

If your page range produces more than one file, they're bundled together and downloaded as a single ZIP archive so you get everything in one action. If your range resolves to just one output file, you'll get that PDF directly.

Does splitting reduce PDF quality?

No. Splitting copies the selected pages exactly as they appear in the source file — text, images and layout are unchanged, and nothing is recompressed. Each output file is simply a subset of the original's pages.

Can I split a password-protected PDF?

Not directly — an encrypted PDF needs to be readable in your browser before its pages can be separated, and that isn't possible without the password. Run it through Unlock PDF first, then split the unlocked copy.
