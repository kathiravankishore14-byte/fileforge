PDF to PPT converter — every page becomes a slide

Someone sends you the PDF of a deck and asks you to present it. Or you need three pages of a report in front of a room. A PDF to PPT converter gets you there: each page of the PDF comes across as a slide in a real .pptx file that PowerPoint, Google Slides and Keynote all open.

How to convert PDF to PPT online free

  1. Add your PDF. Drag it onto the box above or tap Browse files. It is read by your own browser and never uploaded.
  2. Wait while the slides are built. Each page is drawn and added in turn, and the counter tells you which page it is on.
  3. Download the .pptx. It is named after your PDF and ready to open or drop into Google Drive.

What you get, stated plainly

Most converters on this search promise fully editable slides and then deliver text boxes in the wrong places. This tool does something simpler and says so up front:

  • One slide per page. A twelve-page PDF gives you a twelve-slide deck, in order.
  • The layout is exact. Each slide carries a picture of the page, so fonts, charts, logos and spacing look the way they look in the PDF. Nothing reflows.
  • The text is not editable. You cannot click into a heading and retype it. That is the trade for the point above.
  • 16:9 widescreen slides. The standard size, filled edge to edge.
  • A genuine .pptx. Not a renamed PDF — a real PowerPoint file that opens anywhere.

When picture slides are the right answer

  • Presenting a deck you only have as a PDF. The usual case, and the one this handles best — the deck was widescreen to begin with, so it comes back looking right.
  • Dropping pages into a bigger deck. Convert, then copy the slides you need into your own presentation.
  • Showing a document on a projector. Slide view and a clicker beat scrolling a PDF in front of a room.
  • Annotating over the top. Draw arrows, boxes and notes on the slide without touching the original document.
  • Keeping a layout that must not move. Approved artwork, a signed page, a designed report — a picture cannot reflow.

When you want something else instead

If the goal is to rewrite the words rather than show them, PDF to Word gets you editable text to paste into your slides. If you need the pages as plain images to place by hand, use PDF to JPG. If you only want a few pages, extract those pages first. And to go the other way — a deck you want to read as text — try PPT to text.

Free, private and browser-based

  • No signup or email. No account needed to convert your own file.
  • Nothing is uploaded. The PDF is opened and the slides are built on your machine. There is no server copy at all, so there is nothing to delete later.
  • No watermarks or page caps. Convert as many PDFs as you need.
  • Works anywhere. Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iPhone — any modern browser, nothing to install.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PDF to PPT converter free?

Yes. Converting a PDF to PowerPoint here is free with no signup, no trial and no daily cap. Nothing is held back for a paid tier, because the conversion runs on your own machine rather than on a server someone has to pay for.

How do I convert a PDF to PowerPoint online?

Drop your PDF onto the box on this page, or tap Browse files. Each page is rendered and added as a slide, and the .pptx downloads when it is done. There is no upload wait and no queue.

Will the text on the slides be editable?

No, and this is the one thing worth knowing before you start. Each slide holds a picture of the PDF page, so you cannot click into a heading and retype it. In exchange, nothing moves: the layout, fonts and spacing arrive exactly as they look in the PDF, which is the part that usually breaks in converters that try to rebuild editable text. If you need the words back, <a href="/pdf-to-word">convert the PDF to Word</a> and paste them in.

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. Your browser opens the PDF, draws each page and builds the .pptx on your own device. Nothing is sent anywhere, so there is no server copy sitting behind a one-hour deletion timer. For a board pack, a client proposal or anything under an NDA, that is the difference that matters.

What slide size do I get?

Standard 16:9 widescreen, the default in PowerPoint and Google Slides since 2013. Each page image is placed to fill the whole slide edge to edge, with no border.

My PDF is portrait A4 — how will that look?

Every slide is 16:9 widescreen, so a tall portrait page is fitted to that wider shape and will look horizontally stretched. The tool is at its best on PDFs that started life as a deck. If you have a portrait document and want the pages to keep their proportions, <a href="/pdf-to-jpg">export the pages as JPGs</a> and place them on slides yourself.

Can I choose which pages become slides?

Every page in the PDF becomes a slide. To convert only part of a document, <a href="/extract-pdf-pages">pull out the pages you want</a> or <a href="/split-pdf">split the PDF</a> first, then convert the shorter file.

Is there a file size or page limit?

The tool does not impose one. Because the work happens on your device, the real ceiling is your browser's memory, and a long PDF takes longer because every page has to be drawn. A twenty or thirty page deck is no trouble; a few hundred pages is worth splitting up first.

Will the .pptx open in Google Slides and Keynote?

Yes. It is an ordinary PowerPoint file, so PowerPoint, Google Slides, Keynote, LibreOffice Impress and WPS all open it. You can upload it straight to Google Drive without converting anything again.

Can I convert several PDFs at once?

One PDF at a time. Each conversion starts immediately, though, so there is no queue to wait through — pick the next file and go again.

What about a password-protected PDF?

A PDF that asks for a password before it will open cannot be read, so the conversion will fail. Remove the password first — in your PDF reader, or with the <a href="/unlock-pdf">unlock PDF tool</a> if you already know it — and then convert.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. It runs in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge on phones and tablets just as it does on a laptop, with nothing to install. A very long PDF will be slower on a phone, because the same page-drawing work has to happen on a smaller processor.
