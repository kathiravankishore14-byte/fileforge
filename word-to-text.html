Word to Text converter — turn a .docx into clean plain text

A .docx file is a zip archive full of XML: styles, fonts, revision history, relationships between parts. Most of the time you only want the sentences. A Word to Text converter throws away the container and hands you the words in a .txt file you can search, diff, paste, script against or archive without worrying about which version of Word opens it.

How to convert Word to text online free

  1. Add your Word file. Drag the .docx onto the box above, or tap Browse files to pick it. The file is read in your browser — it is never uploaded.
  2. Check the preview. The first few hundred characters appear on the page so you can confirm it is the right document.
  3. Press Convert and download the .txt. You get a plain text file, ready to open in any editor or paste anywhere.

What comes through, and what does not

Plain text is a deliberate reduction, so it helps to know in advance what you will be looking at:

  • All the body text. Paragraphs, headings and list items come through in document order.
  • Table text, without the grid. The words in each cell are read out; rows and columns are not redrawn.
  • UTF-8 encoding. Accented and non-Latin characters are preserved rather than mangled into question marks.
  • No formatting. Bold, italics, fonts, colours, sizes, indents and page breaks are all dropped — that is the point of plain text.
  • No images. Pictures, charts and shapes are not text and are not carried over.
  • No headers, footers or comments. Those live in separate parts of the file and stay behind.

What people use a Word to Text converter for

  • Pasting into something that hates Word formatting. A CMS, a ticket, an email client or a code comment — plain text pastes without dragging in fonts and stray styles.
  • Feeding text to an AI tool. ChatGPT, Claude and similar tools work better with clean text than with a .docx, and converting locally means the document itself never travels.
  • Word counts and proofreading. One flat file is easier to count, grep and read line by line than a styled document.
  • Version comparison. Two .txt files can be diffed with any text tool; two .docx files cannot.
  • Scripting and data work. A .txt input is trivial for Python, shell tools or a spreadsheet import to read.
  • Long-term archiving. Plain text will still open in thirty years without a licence for anything.

.doc, .docx and why the old format needs a detour

The old binary .doc format from pre-2007 Word is a different file structure entirely, and this converter does not read it. The fix takes a moment: open the .doc in Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice Writer, choose Save as and pick .docx, then convert that. Anything saved by Word in the last decade and a half is already .docx.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Word to Text converter free to use?

Yes. Converting a Word document to plain text on OnlineToolsWeb is completely free, with no trial, no credit card and no signup. There are no watermarks and no daily conversion count.

How do I convert a Word document to plain text online?

Drop your .docx file into the box on this page. You get a short preview of the text so you can check you picked the right file, then press Convert and download the .txt. The whole thing happens inside your browser.

Can I see the text before I download it?

Yes. Once the file is loaded, the first few hundred characters are shown on the page. It is a quick sanity check — useful when you have several similarly named drafts and want to be sure which one you are converting.

Which Word formats does it accept?

The modern .docx format, which is what Word, Google Docs and LibreOffice have saved by default for years. If you have an older .doc file, open it and re-save it as .docx first, then bring it back here.

What happens to formatting, images and tables?

Plain text keeps the words and drops everything else. Fonts, sizes, colours, bold, headings and page layout are all removed, and images are not carried over. Text inside a table is read out as lines of text, but the table grid itself is not kept.

Will accented characters and non-English text survive?

Yes. The .txt file is written as UTF-8, so accents, umlauts, Greek, Cyrillic, Indic scripts, CJK characters and symbols come through as they were. Open it in an editor set to UTF-8 and it will look right.

Are headers, footers and comments included?

No. The converter reads the body of the document — paragraphs, lists and table text. Page headers and footers, footnote panes and tracked comments live in separate parts of a .docx file and are not part of the output.

Is my document uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is opened and read by your own browser, and nothing is sent to a server. That matters for contracts, medical letters, HR documents and unpublished manuscripts — there is no copy on someone else's machine to delete later.

Is there a file size limit?

The tool does not impose one. Because the work happens on your device rather than on a server, the practical ceiling is your own browser's memory, and ordinary documents — even long reports with hundreds of pages — are no problem.

Can I convert several Word files at once?

Files are converted one at a time. For a handful of documents that is still quick, since each conversion takes a moment and needs no upload or download wait.

What can I open the .txt file with?

Anything. Notepad on Windows, TextEdit on Mac, any code editor, Word, Google Docs, or a paste straight into an email or a chat box. A .txt file has no proprietary parts, which is exactly why it is a good archive format.

Does the Word to Text converter work on mobile?

Yes. It runs in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge on phones and tablets the same way it does on a laptop, with nothing to install.
