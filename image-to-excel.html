Image to Excel converter — free, private and in your browser

Searching for an image to Excel converter usually means you're staring at a table you can't select: a screenshot someone sent on WhatsApp, a scanned report, a photo of a printed price list. This tool reads the table out of the picture and writes it into an .xlsx spreadsheet you can sort, total and edit — without uploading the image anywhere.

How to convert an image to Excel

  1. Add your image. Drag a JPG, PNG or WebP onto the box above, or tap Browse files to pick it from your device or camera roll.
  2. Let it read the table. The tool finds the rows and columns from the spacing in the image, then reads each cell one at a time. Nothing is sent to a server — the recognition runs on your own device.
  3. Download the spreadsheet. Save the .xlsx and open it in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc or Apple Numbers.
  4. Check it against the image. Text recognition is never perfect. Scan the sheet for odd characters before you rely on the numbers.

  • Your image never leaves your device. Almost every other image-to-Excel site uploads your file, processes it on a server and promises to delete it later. Here there is no upload at all, so an invoice, payslip or medical form stays on your own machine.
  • No signup and no page credits. No account, no email, no "3 free pages this month" meter.

  • No upload queue. There is no server to wait for and no file to send up a slow connection — the work starts the moment you drop the image in.

What converts well — and what doesn't

Being straight about this saves you time. The tool reads printed or typed English text and finds columns from the blank space between them. That means:

  • Works well: screenshots of spreadsheets and web tables, scanned reports, clear photos of printed invoices, marksheets and price lists
  • Works less well: photos taken at an angle, low-light or blurry shots, tables with columns crammed together, faint or coloured text on a busy background
  • Not supported: handwriting, and scripts other than English

Tips for a cleaner conversion

  • Crop to the table. Remove headings, logos and page edges so only the grid is left — this is the single biggest improvement you can make.
  • Shoot square-on. Hold the camera parallel to the page rather than at an angle, or use a scanner app that straightens the page for you.
  • Get the light even. Avoid shadows across the page and glare from a flash.
  • Use the biggest version you have. A full-resolution screenshot reads far better than a small one that's been resized or re-shared through chat.
  • Take a screenshot instead of a photo whenever the table is already on a screen.

Who uses an image to Excel converter

  • Students and researchers lifting a data table out of a scanned paper or a slide photo
  • Teachers turning a printed marksheet into a gradebook
  • Admin and data-entry teams digitising attendance registers, stock lists and old paper records
  • Anyone sent a screenshot of a table when they needed the actual numbers

Image to Excel vs retyping by hand

For five rows, typing is fine. For a fifty-row stock list it isn't, and hand-typed numbers carry their own error rate — transposed digits are easy to make and hard to spot. Converting gives you every row in one pass and leaves you proofreading instead of transcribing, which is faster and easier to check against the original.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Image to Excel converter free to use?

Yes. Converting an image to Excel on OnlineToolsWeb is completely free — no credit card, no page credits and no locked export. Optional premium features may be introduced later for advanced workflows like batch processing, but this tool's core functionality stays free.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The text recognition runs inside your browser on your own device, so the picture is never sent to a server. That matters for the things people usually photograph — invoices, payslips, bank statements, medical forms and marksheets — because none of it leaves your computer or phone.

Do I need to install anything?

No installation needed. Image to Excel runs directly in your browser on any modern desktop or mobile browser — just open this page and use it.

What image formats does it support?

JPG, PNG, WebP and other common image formats. If you have an iPhone HEIC photo, run it through the free HEIC to JPG converter on this site first, then bring the JPG back here.

Can I convert a screenshot of a table to Excel?

Yes, and screenshots usually give the best results. Screen captures are sharp, straight and evenly lit, which is exactly what the text recognition needs. A photo of a printed page works too, as long as it's in focus and shot square-on.

Is there a file size limit or a limit on how many images I can convert?

No. Because everything happens on your own device there is no server quota and no daily cap. Very large images simply take a little longer, since your own processor is doing the reading.

Can it read handwriting?

No. The tool is built for printed or typed text — a scanned table, a screenshot, a photo of a report. Handwritten notes won't convert reliably, so those still need to be typed in by hand.

What languages can it read?

English. The text recognition is set up for English characters, so tables in English convert well while other scripts won't be read accurately. Numbers and standard punctuation are handled as part of that.

How accurate is image to Excel conversion?

It depends almost entirely on the picture. A sharp, straight, well-lit image of a printed table with clear gaps between the columns converts cleanly. A blurry, tilted or low-contrast photo will produce mistakes. Always compare the spreadsheet against the original image before you use the numbers.

Why did my table come out in the wrong columns?

Columns are detected from the visible gaps between them, so anything that blurs those gaps confuses the split — a tilted photo, text that runs into the next column, or extra page content around the table. Crop the image down to just the table, shoot it square-on, and convert again.

Will the file open in Google Sheets?

Yes. You get a standard .xlsx file, so it opens in Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc and Apple Numbers, and every cell is editable.

Does the Image to Excel converter work on mobile?

Yes. It works in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge on phones and tablets, so you can photograph a table and convert it without moving the picture to a computer first.
