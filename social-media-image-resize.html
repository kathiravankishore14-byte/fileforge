Social media image resizer — the right pixels for each platform

Every platform wants a different shape, and the one thing they agree on is that they will crop your picture for you if you do not. Usually badly. A social media image resizer settles it before you upload: you pick the size the platform asks for, and the picture comes back at exactly those pixels.

The four sizes, and what they are for

  • Instagram post — 1080 x 1080. The square feed post. Still the safest shape for anything that needs to read well as a thumbnail.
  • Instagram story — 1080 x 1920. Full-screen vertical, also the right shape for a Reels cover or a WhatsApp status.
  • YouTube thumbnail — 1280 x 720. The 16:9 frame YouTube shows beside every video title.
  • Facebook cover — 820 x 312. The wide banner across the top of a page or profile.

How to resize an image for social media

  1. Add your picture. Drag a JPG, PNG or WebP onto the box above, or tap Browse files. It is read in your browser — never uploaded.
  2. Pick a preset. Instagram post, Instagram story, YouTube thumbnail or Facebook cover.
  3. Press Resize. The image is scaled to cover the frame, trimmed from the centre, and downloaded at the exact pixel size.

Crop to fill, not stretch to fit

There are three ways to force a picture into a different shape, and only one of them looks right. Stretching distorts everything — faces go wide, circles go oval. Adding bars keeps the whole picture but wastes the frame and looks like a mistake on a feed. This tool does the third: it scales the image up until it covers the frame completely, then trims the overhang from the edges, keeping the middle. Proportions stay true and the frame is filled. The cost is the edges, so if your subject is not near the centre, crop it roughly yourself first and then resize.

Getting a sharp result

  • Start big. Upscaling invents pixels and softens the picture. Use the original, not a version already shrunk for a chat app.
  • Mind the subject's position. The crop is centred, so a face or logo at the far edge may be trimmed.
  • Check text. Small lettering that survives at full size can blur once the picture is scaled down to 820 pixels wide.
  • Compress last. Resize first, then compress if the file needs to be smaller — doing it the other way round wastes quality.

  • No daily limit. Resize as many pictures as you want.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the social media image resizer free?

Yes. Resizing here is free with no signup, no watermark and no daily limit. The work happens on your own machine, so there is nothing to charge for.

What sizes can I resize to?

Four presets, each the size the platform actually asks for: Instagram post at 1080 x 1080 pixels, Instagram story at 1080 x 1920, YouTube thumbnail at 1280 x 720 and Facebook cover at 820 x 312. Pick one from the dropdown and press Resize.

Does it stretch or squash my photo?

No. The picture is scaled until it covers the target size and then trimmed to fit, so the proportions never change — a face stays a face rather than turning oval. The trade is that some of the edges are cropped away.

Part of my picture got cut off — why?

Because the shape you started with and the shape you asked for are different, and something has to give. The crop is taken from the centre. If the part you care about sits off to one side, <a href="/crop-image">crop the image yourself first</a>, roughly to the right shape, then run it through here.

What if I need a size that is not on the list?

Use <a href="/resize-image">resize image</a> and type any width and height you like — that covers LinkedIn banners, X headers, Pinterest pins, TikTok covers and anything else a platform changes its mind about next month.

Does it change my file format?

No. A PNG comes back as a PNG and a JPG as a JPG. If you want to switch formats, <a href="/convert-image-format">convert the image</a> before or after resizing.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is loaded, cropped and re-saved by your own browser, and nothing is sent to a server. Nobody else ever holds a copy of the picture — which is worth having for client work, product shots before launch, or photographs of people.

My image is smaller than the size I picked — what happens?

It is scaled up to fill the frame, and it will look softer for it, because the extra pixels have to be invented. Start from the largest version you have. For a YouTube thumbnail at 1280 x 720, anything under about 1280 pixels wide will look noticeably soft on a big screen.

Can I resize several photos at once?

One image at a time. Each resize is instant, though — there is no upload and no queue — so a set of posts goes quickly.

Will Instagram crop my picture again after I upload it?

Not at these sizes. A 1080 x 1080 square and a 1080 x 1920 story are both shapes Instagram accepts as they are, so it has no reason to trim anything. Most re-cropping happens when people upload an odd shape and let the app decide.

Is there a file size limit?

The tool does not impose one. Because the work happens on your device, the practical ceiling is your browser's memory, and ordinary camera and phone photos are nowhere near it.

The file came out larger than the original — can I shrink it?

Yes. Resizing changes the pixel dimensions, not how hard the file is compressed, so a re-saved image can end up bigger. Run it through <a href="/compress-image">compress image</a> afterwards to bring the file size down.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, and that is often where it makes most sense. It runs in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge on phones and tablets with nothing to install, so you can size a photo for a story on the device that took it.
