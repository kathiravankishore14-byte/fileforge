Crop Image lets you drag out exactly the part of a photo you want to keep and discard the rest. It's for the times a photo has too much around the edges — cutting a subject out of a wider shot, trimming a scanned document down to just its content, or removing distracting background before using the image somewhere else.

How this tool works

Your image is loaded into an interactive crop box built with Cropper.js, a browser-based cropping library. When the image first appears, the crop box is already positioned covering about 80% of it, so you have an immediate starting selection to fine-tune rather than an empty canvas. You can drag any edge or corner to resize the box freely — there's no fixed aspect ratio — or drag inside it to move the whole selection around. When you apply the crop, only the pixels inside that box are kept, redrawn onto a new canvas at their original resolution and saved in the same file format you uploaded.

How to crop an image

  1. Drop your photo on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Drag the crop box's edges and corners to select the area you want to keep.
  3. Drag inside the box to reposition it if needed.
  4. Click Apply Crop and download the result, cropped locally in your browser.

Privacy and local processing

Your photo stays on your device throughout. The crop preview and the final cropping both run inside your browser using the Canvas API — nothing is uploaded, so OnlineToolsWeb never sees the image. Once the page has loaded, cropping keeps working the same way offline.

Practical limitations

The crop box is freeform rather than locked to preset ratios like square or 16:9, so matching an exact ratio takes a careful manual drag rather than a one-click preset. Because cropping keeps pixels at their original resolution, a very small crop area from a large photo will still be high resolution, but a small crop area from an already-small photo can end up quite low resolution. Very large, high-resolution source photos take a little longer to load into the interactive crop view and can use more memory on phones or older devices.

Common ways people use this tool

Common uses include tightening a photo around its subject by trimming excess background, cutting a scanned page down to just its printed content, removing another person or distracting object from the edge of a photo, and preparing an image at a specific framing before resizing it further for a website or social post.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I crop to a specific aspect ratio, or is it freeform?

The crop box is freeform — you can drag its edges and corners to any shape and size you like rather than being locked to a fixed ratio like square or 16:9. This gives you full control over exactly which part of the image you keep.

What determines the initial crop box size?

When your image loads, the crop box starts out covering about 80% of it, centered, so you immediately have a sensible starting selection to adjust rather than an empty box you have to draw from scratch. From there you can drag any edge or corner to resize it, or drag inside the box to move it.

Does cropping change the image format?

No — the cropped image is saved in the same format you uploaded. A JPG stays a JPG, a PNG stays a PNG, and so on.

Can I crop a very large photo?

Yes, there's no hard-coded size limit. Very large, high-resolution photos take a little more memory and time to load into the crop view and process than a typical web-sized image.

Does cropping reduce image quality?

Cropping itself doesn't degrade quality — it simply keeps the pixels inside your selected area and discards the rest, at their original resolution. Quality loss would only come from separately compressing the result afterward, which this tool doesn't do automatically.

Does the crop box work with touch on a phone or tablet?

Yes, the crop box responds to touch the same way it responds to mouse drags on desktop, so you can resize and move it with your finger.
