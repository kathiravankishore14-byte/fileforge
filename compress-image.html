Compress Image reduces a photo's file size by re-encoding it as a JPG at a quality level you choose, with a live estimate of the result shown before you commit. It's built for shrinking photos that are too large to email, attach, or upload — without needing a separate app or guessing at settings.

How this tool works

You choose a quality level — Low, Medium, or Extraordinary — from a dropdown, and the tool draws your image onto an in-memory canvas and re-encodes it as a JPG at that quality using the browser's own image-encoding engine. As soon as you change the quality setting, the tool actually performs that compression right away and measures the real file size it produces, showing you that true result — not a simulated guess — before you decide to download. When you click Compress, the same process runs once more and hands you the finished file, always saved as a .jpg regardless of the format you uploaded.

How to compress an image

  1. Drop your photo on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Choose a quality level and watch the live estimated size update.
  3. Adjust the quality if you want a different size-versus-quality balance.
  4. Click Compress and download the result — always saved as a JPG.

Privacy and local processing

Your photo stays on your device the whole time. Both the live size estimate and the final compression happen inside your browser using the Canvas API — nothing is uploaded to a server, so OnlineToolsWeb never sees the image. Once the page has loaded, compression keeps working the same way offline.

Practical limitations

The output is always a JPG, so any transparency in a PNG source image is lost — if you need to keep transparency, Convert Image Format lets you pick a different output format instead. Because JPEG compression is lossy, image quality does gradually decrease as you move toward the Low setting, and how noticeable that is depends on the photo itself — busy, detailed images tend to hide compression artifacts better than smooth, flat-color images. Very large or high-resolution photos take longer to redraw and re-encode each time you change the quality, since the estimate is a real compression pass rather than a shortcut calculation.

Common ways people use this tool

People typically use this to shrink a phone photo down so it fits an email attachment limit, to speed up a website or online store by reducing product photo file sizes, to prepare images for a file upload form with a strict size cap, and to reduce a batch of photos before adding them to a document or presentation without visibly hurting their appearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does compression turn my PNG into a JPG?

Yes. This tool always saves its output as a JPG, regardless of the format you uploaded, since JPEG's lossy compression is what actually shrinks the file. If your PNG relies on transparency, that transparency will be lost — JPG doesn't support it.

Why does the estimated size change as I move the quality dropdown?

The estimate isn't a guess — every time you change the quality setting, the tool actually compresses your image at that setting right there in your browser and measures the real resulting size, so what you see before downloading is the true output size, not an approximation.

Which quality level should I choose?

Medium is selected by default and suits most everyday photos. Choose Low when file size matters most, such as attaching several photos to an email. Choose Extraordinary when you want the smallest possible reduction in visual quality, accepting a smaller size saving in return.

What happens to transparent images?

Since the output is always JPG, any transparent areas in your original image will be filled in rather than staying transparent. If you need to keep transparency, this tool isn't the right fit — Convert Image Format lets you choose a format that supports it.

Is there a file size limit?

There's no hard-coded limit, but very large or very high-resolution photos take a bit longer to compress and re-estimate, since the whole image is redrawn and re-encoded in your browser's memory each time.

Can I compress images on my phone?

Yes, the tool and its live size estimate both work the same way in a mobile browser. Very large photos may take a little longer to process on a phone than on a desktop.
