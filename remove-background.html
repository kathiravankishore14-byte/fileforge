Remove Background automatically separates a photo's subject from its background using an AI model, giving you a transparent cutout ready to drop onto a new background or use as-is. It's built for the everyday need to isolate a product, a person, or an object from whatever's behind it — without a manual selection tool or a design app.

How this tool works

When you drop in a photo, the tool loads ORMBG, an open-source AI segmentation model trained specifically on photos of people, directly into your browser and runs it on your device to identify which pixels belong to the subject and which belong to the background. It runs at full precision by default for the sharpest, least noisy cutout; if that version fails to load, the tool automatically falls back to a smaller, quantized version instead, so you still get a result. Once the cutout is generated, a softening pass is applied to the edge of the transparent area — gently blending the boundary between subject and background rather than leaving a hard edge — before you're shown a before/after comparison slider and the option to swap in a new solid color or background image.

How to remove a background

  1. Drop your photo on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Wait while the AI model loads and processes your image locally in your browser.
  3. Use the before/after slider to check the cutout, and optionally swap in a new background.
  4. Download your result with a transparent or replaced background.

Privacy and local processing

Your photo is never uploaded. The AI model itself is downloaded once so it can run, but the actual image you're processing stays on your device — the cutout, edge softening, and any background swap all happen inside your browser. OnlineToolsWeb never sees or stores your photo.

Practical limitations

Because the AI model runs on your device rather than a server, the first cutout in a session includes a brief model download and startup delay, and processing takes longer on older or lower-powered phones and laptops than on a fast modern desktop. No automatic background remover is perfect on every photo — fine, wispy detail like loose hair strands, transparent or reflective objects, and low-contrast edges between subject and background are the situations most likely to need manual touch-up afterward, even with the edge-softening pass applied. If the quantized model can't load in your browser, the tool automatically retries with the full-precision version rather than stopping outright.

Common ways people use this tool

Common uses include isolating a product photo onto a plain white or transparent background for an online store listing, cutting a person out of a photo for a headshot or ID-style image, preparing a subject for a marketing graphic or social media post with a custom background, and pulling a logo or object off a busy background for reuse in other designs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this tool upload my photo to a server to remove the background?

No. The background removal runs entirely inside your browser using an AI model that's downloaded once and executed locally on your device. Your photo itself is never sent anywhere — the cutout happens on your own computer or phone.

Which AI model does it use?

It uses ORMBG, an open-source segmentation model trained specifically on photos of people, running at full precision for the sharpest, least noisy cutout. A smaller, quantized version is used as an automatic fallback if the full-precision one fails to load, at some cost to edge quality.

What happens if the AI model can't load?

The tool first tries the full-precision version of the model, then a smaller, quantized version if that one fails to load. If both attempts fail, the tool shows an error rather than a broken or blank result, and you can try again.

How are difficult edges, like hair, handled?

After the AI produces its initial cutout, the tool applies a softening pass to the edge of the transparent area, gently blending the boundary rather than leaving a hard, jagged line. This helps fine detail like hair and soft edges look more natural, though very fine or wispy strands remain one of the harder cases for any automatic background remover to get perfectly.

Will the AI always produce a perfect cutout?

No automatic background remover is perfect on every photo. Clear subjects against a simple, contrasting background tend to come out cleanest. Complex edges, transparent or reflective objects, and low-contrast boundaries between the subject and background are the situations most likely to need a manual touch-up afterward.

Can I use this on my phone, and will it be slow?

Yes, it works on mobile browsers. Since the AI model runs on your own device, the first use in a session includes a brief model download and startup delay, and processing can take a bit longer on an older or lower-powered phone than on a desktop.
