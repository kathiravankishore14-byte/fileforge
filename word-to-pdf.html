Accepts: Word (.docx) files

Word to PDF converts a .docx file's text into a new PDF document, built fresh in your browser. It's for the times a PDF is simply the format you need to submit, share, or archive a document in — like turning a written draft into a PDF for a form upload or an email attachment that keeps its content locked in place.

How this tool works

Your .docx file is read using mammoth, a library that extracts the plain text content from a Word document. That raw text is then laid out into a new PDF using jsPDF: each line is measured and wrapped to fit the page width, written into the document from the top margin downward, and once the text reaches near the bottom of a page, the tool automatically starts a fresh page and keeps going until the whole document's text has been placed. This is a text-extraction-and-rebuild approach — mammoth deliberately strips all formatting when it pulls the text out, so the PDF contains your document's words, paginated cleanly, rather than a pixel-for-pixel copy of the original page design.

  1. Drop your .docx file on the box above, or click to browse and select it.
  2. Click the button to start — no settings to adjust.
  3. The document's text is extracted and laid out into a new PDF locally in your browser.
  4. Download the finished PDF.

Privacy and local processing

Your Word document never leaves your device. Both the text extraction and the PDF layout happen inside your browser's JavaScript engine, so nothing is uploaded and OnlineToolsWeb never sees the file's contents. Once the page has loaded, conversion keeps working the same way offline.

Practical limitations

Because only plain text is extracted, formatting from your original document — bold and italic text, fonts, colors, embedded images, tables, and bulleted or numbered list styling — is not reproduced in the PDF; you get the wording, laid out as plain paragraphs. Page breaks in the output are based purely on how much text fits per page, not on any page breaks in your original Word file. Only .docx files are supported; an older .doc file needs to be saved as .docx first. Very long documents take proportionally longer to lay out, since every line is measured and placed individually.

Common ways people use this tool

People typically use this to turn a written draft or letter into a PDF for a form or portal that only accepts PDFs, to convert meeting notes or a report into a format that won't shift when opened on someone else's computer, to create a simple PDF copy of a document for archiving, and to quickly get text out of Word into a shareable PDF without worrying about matching fonts on the recipient's end.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the PDF keep my Word document's formatting, like bold text or images?

No. This tool extracts the plain text from your .docx file and lays it out fresh in a new PDF — bold, italics, fonts, colors, embedded images, and tables from the original aren't carried over. What you get is the document's wording, cleanly paginated as a PDF.

What file types can I convert?

This tool accepts .docx files, the format modern Microsoft Word and Google Docs exports use. Older .doc files aren't supported directly — save or export the document as .docx first, then convert it here.

How does the tool decide where to break pages?

Text is laid out line by line starting near the top of the page, and once the content reaches near the bottom margin, the tool automatically starts a new page and continues. This is based purely on how much text fits, not on any page breaks that existed in your original Word document.

Will tables or bulleted lists convert correctly?

Tables, bullet points, and other structured formatting aren't preserved as their own elements — since only plain text is extracted, list items and table cell contents come through as ordinary text lines rather than a formatted list or grid.

Is there a limit on how long a document I can convert?

There's no hard-coded page limit — the tool keeps adding pages to the PDF for as much text as your document contains. Very long documents simply take a little longer to lay out and produce a PDF with more pages.

Can I convert Word to PDF on my phone?

Yes, it works the same way in a mobile browser. Very long documents may take a little longer to process on a phone than on a desktop.
